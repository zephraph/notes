Smartphones have changed our society. The internet enabled us to all be connected, but smartphones enabled us to _always_ be connected. It's an ever present window from our world into the digital world, ever blurring the lines between both. With so much of the world's information available at our fingertips why does it often feel like we're worse off for it?

## Considering the modern smartphone

There was a time when smartphones were a business tool and the most engagement we got through our phones was SMS or playing MP3s. This changed in a real way when the iPhone released... or rather, later when 3rd party apps came about. 

Smartphones have become a general purpose computer much like our desktops. Like a desktop it has applications that can be published by any developer in the world[^1]. This gives 3rd parties a direct connection from their servers to a device that's rarely out of reach. In the current technological environment 3rd parties are economically incentivised to drive engagement. The more you engage, the more valuable you are to them. Thus slews of VC funds flow into organizations hyper optimizing on user engagement and retention. Eyeballs on screens. Apps like tiktok are what fall out of that environment. An app so addictive and engaging that the hours can slip by without you knowing (or caring).

Surely there's a better alternative than this. Surely, for those of us want to escape the shackles of digitial solitude there are alternatives that empower instead of detract... Right?

## Mitigating Distraction

### Opt-out

I've oft heard something along the lines of _problems caused by technology can't be solved by technology_ and there's some merit to that. The best way to not get addicted to your smartphone is to not have a smartphone. To me this is... unfortunate. To be deprived of capabilities that enhance my life because I can't prevent the device from consuming life instead. 

### Paired down experiences

As with all things in life, a little bit of discipline can go a long way. Why not just... delete all your distracting apps? Turn off notifications that don't add value to your life. Hide things away in a folder that's hard to get to. Install utility apps or shortcuts to add friction to the experience to make it less attractive. Do what you can with what you have. This is probably the best option we have today, but it feels to me like I'm setting up an adversarial relationship with my device. This is a Sisyphean task that doesn't scale beyond an individual. We can do better.  

### Minimal phones

The market for minimal smartphones phones is still niche but it's growing. 

Here's a few notable options (but there are many more):

- [Mudita Pure](https://store.mudita.com/mudita-pure-minimalist-phone-pebble-gray)
	- This is probably one of my favorites in the category currently. Kind of makes me think of one of those old school Nokia phones but sleeker and with an e-ink screen. It has a custom, open source operation system too which is a huge boon.
- [Punkt MP02](https://www.punkt.ch/en/products/mp02-4g-mobile-phone/)
- [Light Phone](https://www.thelightphone.com/products)

While these are nice, I feel like they miss the mark. Most are really focused on slimming the phone down to what it was in the early 2000s. A simple handset with SMS, an alarm, and maybe some music, but nothing else. These are great and I hope folks buy them and find them helpful. I'm glad products in this category exist. Still, I want to step forward into a better technological future not regress to a good enough state. 

### Build a better way

Maybe it's time to pave a new path. The experiments are already happening with companies like [Blloc](https://www.blloc.com/) trying to rethink the default experience. How can we preserve the things that are incredibly valuable like easy access to information, navigation, camera, contacts, etc while removing or reducing distractions. Can we then go further and make the device _more_ helpful. Key off of built in contexts like location, time, calendar events, etc to enable us to focus more on what we want and less on the device itself. 

## Envisioning the Smartphone from first principles

The rest of the notes in this series is going to be on this topic. I'm unsatisfied with the options above and honestly I feel like our current platforms (iOS, Android) have incentive structures that actively combat our ability to have a healthy, fulfilling relationship with our phones. We need to use them less but get more out of the time when we do use them.
