If we consider [[Alexander Obenauer|Alexander]]'s [[Graph OS]] for a moment, there's a notion that there are items in the graph that are provided by services or the user. Items, objects, or whatever you want to call them have names and a schema. They contain types that can contain other types with all of those types _also_ having names. If then you consider that the system has both user generated and 3rd party items flowing into it, how does one avoid name conflicts and normalize schema data?

I think [[unison]] has something to teach us here. [[Unison]] hashes its code based on its structure and types and then stores it in a database. It calls this [[content addressability]]. The interesting properties of [[content addressability]] is that names (like function or variable names) can be changed with little to no consequence in the wider system. A package that imports your package and calls a function depends on its _hash_, not its name. 

Thinking about the [[GraphOS]] with [[content addressability]] then, we can have an email item provided by the gmail service and a different email provided by the fastmail service that could both be named email with no consequence. Likely you'd want to have these services provide items under a parent node named after the service like `gmail.email` and `fastmail.email`. You could have your rendition of an email store either at the root or under a personal namespace that is an aggregation of these other two nodes.
